2005 · A basement lab in Philadelphia

Fund the mRNA Lab

Modified mRNA in fat bubbles will make vaccines in days. Fund it and build the factories now.

The biochemist had spent fifteen years on an idea that nobody wanted to fund. Her grant applications came back rejected. She had been moved down a rung at the university. Her idea was that you could inject a cell with a message, a strand of mRNA, and the cell would follow its instructions and make a protein. A medicine, or a vaccine.

The problem was that the body attacked the message as an invader. Then she and a colleague found a fix: swap one of the letters in the message for a slightly modified version, and the alarm stays quiet.

They published it in 2005. Hardly anyone noticed.

I came back to make people notice. I told funders that within fifteen years a new virus would spread around the world, and that this method, wrapped in tiny fat bubbles, would produce a working vaccine design within days of the virus's genetic sequence being published.

I asked for three things: fund the lab; build manufacturing that can scale; and run the early safety trials now, in calm years, so that when the crisis comes nothing is waiting on paperwork.

Most funders smiled politely. One, who had lost a parent to the flu, wrote a cheque. The lab moved upstairs.

The ask

  1. Fund modified-mRNA research now, at scale.
  2. Develop the lipid nanoparticles that carry it.
  3. Build factories and supply chains in calm years.
  4. Prepare the trial and approval process for a pandemic.
And for myself: a good life in the 2000s

Mine a few bitcoin, and guard the key

In January 2009 a strange new kind of money appears that anyone can mine on a laptop. In 2010 someone pays 10,000 of them for two pizzas. I'd mine a few hundred, write the key on paper in two safe places, and also sell my house in 2006.

  • Mine bitcoin in 2009; back up the key, offline, twice.
  • Sell property in 2006; buy back in 2009.
  • Don't take an adjustable-rate mortgage.

Bitcoin's first block was mined on 3 January 2009; on 22 May 2010 a programmer paid 10,000 BTC for two pizzas. US house prices peaked around 2006. Hindsight, not advice.

A note on why

mRNA vaccines arrived in 2020 just in time, but they were nearly lost to a decade of indifference. Preparing earlier would have meant vaccines sooner and in more places.

Sometimes the best thing to carry back is not an invention, but a name: who to fund.

  • Katalin Karikó and Drew Weissman published their finding on modified nucleosides in mRNA in 2005, and won the 2023 Nobel Prize for it.
  • Karikó's work was repeatedly turned down for funding in the 1990s.
  • COVID-19 mRNA vaccine designs were completed within days of the virus's sequence being published in January 2020.

Runners-up for this era: 'stockpile masks and tests'; 'the financial crisis is coming'; better email security.

The funder and the scene are illustrative; the narrator is invented. Karikó and Weissman's work and its history are real. This is a story about history, not medical, legal or safety advice.
