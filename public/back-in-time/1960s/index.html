November, 1965 · Washington, D.C.

The Curve on Mauna Loa

The carbon curve keeps rising and the planet warms. Start building clean power now.

The report was hundreds of pages long, about pesticides and sewage and detergent foam on rivers. In one appendix, a group of scientists noted calmly that the carbon dioxide from burning fossil fuels was building up in the air, and that by the year 2000 it might change the climate in ways that could be deleterious.

That appendix was read by almost nobody. I had come back to make it the first page.

I had a chart, drawn on graph paper, extending the line from the station on Mauna Loa that had been measuring since 1958. Its zigzag climbed with the seasons and the years. I drew it on, past 1965, past 1990, past 2020, to where I knew it would be: higher than at any time in human history.

I knew what the scientists in 1965 couldn't be sure of. About a degree and a quarter of warming by the 2020s. Heat waves, fires, the ice going. And I knew the cheap answers that were coming: solar panels that would fall in price by more than 99%, wind turbines, batteries, and a fleet of nuclear plants that could have been built much earlier.

A congressman's aide listened for twenty minutes, then asked if the warming might be good for farming. Some of the scientists listened longer.

You can't change a world in an afternoon. But you can make sure a curve is on the first page and not in an appendix.

The first page

  1. Burning fossil fuels raises CO₂ every year: measure it, publish it.
  2. By the 2020s it will warm the planet by over a degree.
  3. Start building clean power now: nuclear, and fund solar, wind and batteries.
  4. Every decade of delay makes the job bigger.
And for myself: a good life in the 1960s

Skip the tan

Everyone wants a deep tan, and sunbathing with baby oil is fashionable. Sunburn now means skin damage and cancer risk later. A hat, shade and long sleeves are the secret to looking and feeling well at seventy.

  • Hats, shade and shirts in strong sun.
  • Never burn; avoid the midday sun.
  • And don't start smoking.

UV exposure and sunburn are major risk factors for skin cancers, including melanoma; the SPF rating system came into use in the 1970s. Hindsight, not advice.

A note on why

Climate change is the clearest case where the science arrived in time and the action didn't. Carrying the information back is easy; carrying the urgency is the hard part.

A head start of even twenty years on clean energy would have changed the whole curve.

  • Continuous CO₂ measurements at Mauna Loa began in 1958 (the Keeling Curve).
  • A 1965 report by the US President's Science Advisory Committee warned that CO₂ from fossil fuels could measurably change the climate by 2000.
  • By the early 2020s, global average temperature was about 1.1–1.2 °C above pre-industrial levels.
  • The price of solar modules fell by over 99% between the 1970s and the 2020s.

Runners-up for this era: 'thalidomide causes birth defects'; seat belts in every car; the internet's security model.

The narrator and the congressman's aide are invented. The 1965 report, the Keeling Curve and the warming figures are real. This is a story about history, not medical, legal or safety advice.
