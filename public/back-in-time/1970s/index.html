1978 · A blood bank and a clinic, New York

Screen the Blood

A silent virus is spreading through blood and sex. Screen blood products and use condoms.

Nobody knew it was there. That was the whole problem. A virus that hides in a person for years, with no symptoms at all, while it quietly destroys the immune system and passes, by blood and by sex, to the next person and the next.

By the time doctors noticed in 1981, clusters of rare pneumonias and cancers in young men, it had already spread across continents. Thousands of people with haemophilia, who depended on clotting factor pooled from many donors, had already been infected through their medicine.

I couldn't give anyone a test for it; that wouldn't exist until 1985. But I could give them the facts, without fear or blame. It's a virus, not a punishment. It travels in blood and sexual fluids, not in a handshake or a shared cup. Condoms stop it. Clean needles stop it. Heat-treating clotting factor kills it.

The blood bank director listened, because a pharmacologist friend had been worrying about donated plasma for other reasons already. The clinic I volunteered at started handing out condoms with every visit, and a community newsletter printed it on its front page.

It was not enough to stop a pandemic. Nothing one person says is. But a few years of warning, given kindly, would have meant a few years less of silent spread, and that is a great many people.

The newsletter

  1. There is a virus that can hide for years with no symptoms.
  2. It spreads through blood and sex, not everyday contact.
  3. Use condoms. Never share needles.
  4. Blood banks: heat-treat clotting factor and screen donors.

A note on why

HIV spread silently for a decade before it was recognised, and tens of millions of people have died of AIDS. The early lessons of prevention were simple and cheap; it was the time lost that was expensive.

Information is the right tool here, delivered without stigma, because fear makes people hide.

  • Genetic studies suggest HIV reached the United States around 1970 and spread undetected through the decade.
  • The CDC's first report of what became known as AIDS was published in June 1981. An HIV blood test became available in 1985.
  • Many people with haemophilia were infected through contaminated clotting factor before heat treatment and screening were adopted.
  • Around 40 million people have died of AIDS-related illnesses since the start of the epidemic.
