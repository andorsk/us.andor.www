Summer, 1908 · A cotton-mill village in South Carolina

Soak the Corn in Lime

Pellagra is a diet disease. Treat corn with lime, and add beans, milk or meat.

In the mill village they called it the red sickness, or just 'the pellagra', and they were ashamed of it. It began each spring: a red, rough rash on the backs of the hands and the neck, where the sun touched. Then the stomach trouble. Then, in the worst cases, the mind went, and some ended in the asylum.

Doctors from the state came and went. They said it was a germ, or spoiled corn, or something in the water, and they looked at the mill families as though the families had brought it on themselves.

The families ate what the company store sold cheapest: cornmeal, molasses and fatback pork. Every day, all winter.

I knew two things that were three decades away. First, the sickness came from what was missing from the plate, not from what was on it. Second, the people who had eaten corn for thousands of years, in Mexico, never got it, because they always soaked and cooked their corn with lime or wood ash before grinding it.

So I showed the women on our row how to make hominy the old way, with slaked lime from the builder's yard, and we planted beans and greens in the yard of every house that would let us, and bartered for milk. The rashes faded by midsummer. The sickness didn't come back with the spring.

A doctor from Columbia came to see why our row was well. I hope he wrote it down.

For the corn

  1. Simmer dried corn in water with slaked lime (or wood ash) and let it soak overnight.
  2. Rinse well, then cook or grind it: hominy and masa.
  3. Eat it with beans, greens, eggs, milk or meat.
  4. A plate of only meal, molasses and fatback causes the sickness.
And for myself: a good life in the 1900s

Be out of San Francisco on 18 April 1906

At 5:12 in the morning the ground shakes for about a minute, and then the fires burn for days. Much of the city is destroyed. I'd take a trip that week, insure everything I owned with a sound company, and come back to help rebuild.

  • Be away from San Francisco in mid-April 1906.
  • Insure your property well beforehand.
  • Otherwise: a quiet life in Los Angeles, where land is still cheap.

The 1906 earthquake and fire destroyed much of San Francisco; around 3,000 people are estimated to have died. Hindsight, not advice.

A note on why

Pellagra hit hundreds of thousands of poor Americans in the early 1900s, and many died, while scientists searched for a germ. It was a missing vitamin, niacin, and the cure was food.

Mesoamerican cooks had solved it thousands of years earlier: treating corn with an alkali (nixtamalisation) releases its niacin.

  • Between about 1906 and 1940, pellagra caused an estimated 3 million cases and around 100,000 deaths in the American South.
  • From 1914 Joseph Goldberger showed it was caused by diet, not infection. Niacin was identified as the missing nutrient in 1937.
  • Nixtamalisation, cooking corn with lime or ash, has been practised in Mesoamerica for millennia.

Runners-up for this era: seat belts (too early: cars were rare); pasteurised milk; 'smoking causes cancer'.

The village, the families and the doctor are invented. The epidemic, Goldberger's work and nixtamalisation are real. This is a story about history, not medical, legal or safety advice.
