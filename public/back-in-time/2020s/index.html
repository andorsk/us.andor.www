January, 2020 · An office, a school, a restaurant

It's in the Air

The new coronavirus spreads through the air. Ventilate, filter, mask, and meet outside.

In January, the advice was to wash your hands. It was good advice, and it was the wrong virus for it.

I came back three weeks, not three centuries, and I had one sentence: it's in the air. Not only in big droplets that fall within a few feet, but in tiny particles that float in a room like smoke, and build up when the windows are closed.

So the advice changes. Open the windows. Run the ventilation, with fresh air, not recycled. Put a filter in every classroom: a box fan with four furnace filters taped to it will do. Wear a well-fitting mask indoors with strangers. Eat outside when you can.

Washing your hands still helps against plenty of things, I said. It just isn't how this one moves.

Some people listened early. A few schools bought filters in February. A restaurant moved its tables onto the pavement before anyone told it to. The official guidance took over a year to say clearly what aerosol scientists had been saying since the spring.

Of all the things on this list, this is the one most of us lived through. Imagine if we'd known on the first day.

Day one

  1. It spreads through the air, like smoke. Indoors is the risk.
  2. Open windows; bring in fresh air.
  3. Filter the air: a box fan with four furnace filters works.
  4. Wear a well-fitting mask indoors; meet outside.

A note on why

COVID-19 killed millions of people. Early messaging focused on surfaces and large droplets, and acknowledgement of airborne spread came slowly. Cheap measures like ventilation and filtration were underused for months.

It's the closest of all these eras, and that's the point: we don't need a time machine to learn the lesson for the next one.

  • In March 2020 the WHO publicly stated that COVID-19 was not airborne; it acknowledged airborne transmission more fully in 2021.
  • Aerosol scientists wrote to the WHO in July 2020 urging it to recognise airborne spread.
  • The Corsi–Rosenthal box, a fan and four filters, was designed in 2020 as a low-cost air cleaner.
  • The WHO estimates that COVID-19 caused millions of excess deaths worldwide.

Runners-up for this era: mRNA earlier; 'test widely, early'; 'prepare stockpiles'.

The narrator is invented; the schools and restaurant are composites. The WHO timeline, the aerosol scientists' letter and the Corsi–Rosenthal box are real. This is a story about history, not medical, legal or safety advice.
