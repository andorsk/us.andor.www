Winter, 1936 · A hospital chest ward in Ohio

Count the Smokers

Cigarettes cause lung cancer and heart disease. Prove it with a simple count.

Everybody smoked. The patients smoked in bed, the nurses smoked in the sluice room, the doctors smoked on rounds. The magazines had cigarettes in every advertisement, and some of them had doctors in white coats holding them.

Lung cancer had been so rare when the senior surgeon trained that he had seen perhaps one case as a student. Now there were three on his ward. He thought it might be the influenza of 1918, or the motor cars, or the new tarred roads.

I asked him to do something very dull. Every time a patient came in with lung cancer, ask how much they smoked. Every time a patient came in with something else, a broken leg, a hernia, ask the same. Write it in two columns.

It took him a year. When he added it up, he went out onto the fire escape and lit a cigarette, looked at it, and threw it into the yard.

Nearly all of the lung cancer patients were heavy smokers. Far fewer of the others were. The column was so lopsided that he checked his arithmetic three times.

He published it in a small journal, and some people laughed, and the tobacco companies wrote letters. But it was the right count, fourteen years early.

The count

  1. For every lung cancer patient, record how much they smoke.
  2. Do the same for patients admitted for other reasons, of similar age.
  3. Compare the two columns.
  4. Then stop smoking. Start with the doctors.

A note on why

Smoking killed an estimated 100 million people in the 20th century. Lung cancer went from a medical curiosity to the leading cause of cancer death, and the evidence that would prove why took decades to be accepted.

The proof that eventually convinced the world was a case-control study: a careful count. Any curious doctor in the 1930s could have done it.

  • Lung cancer was rare around 1900 and became common as cigarette smoking spread.
  • German researchers reported a link in 1939; Richard Doll and Austin Bradford Hill's 1950 case-control study made the case widely.
  • The US Surgeon General's report of 1964 officially linked smoking to lung cancer.
  • The World Health Organization estimates tobacco killed about 100 million people in the 20th century.

Runners-up for this era: seat belts; penicillin's purification; sulfa drugs.

The ward and the surgeon are invented. The rise of lung cancer, the 1939 and 1950 studies and the 1964 report are real. This is a story about history, not medical, legal or safety advice.
